# Unit 40: Much, Many, A Lot (of)

## Learning Objectives
- Use "much" in negative sentences and questions with uncountable nouns
- Use "many" in negative sentences and questions with plural countable nouns
- Use "a lot of" with countable and uncountable nouns in affirmative and negative sentences and questions
- Use "a lot" (without "of") when no noun follows

## Much

We often use "much" in negative sentences and questions, with uncountable nouns.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Uncountable (negative) | I haven't got much lemonade. |
| Uncountable (question) | Have you got much water? |

## Many

We often use "many" in negative sentences and questions, with plural countable nouns.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Plural countable (negative) | I haven't got many chips. |
| Plural countable (question) | Have you got many eggs? |

## A lot of

We often use "a lot of" in affirmative and negative sentences and in questions, with countable and uncountable nouns.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Countable (affirmative) | I have got a lot of apples. |
| Countable (negative) | I haven't got a lot of tomatoes. |
| Uncountable (question) | Have you got a lot of water? |

## A lot (without "of")

We use "of" when "a lot" comes before a noun. But when there is no noun after "a lot" we do not use "of".

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Before a noun | I've got a lot of bananas. |
| No noun after | Are there any bananas? Yes, there are a lot. |
