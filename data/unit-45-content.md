# Unit 45: Phrasal Verbs

## Learning Objectives
- Learn 25 common phrasal verbs and their meanings
- Use phrasal verbs correctly in sentences
- Understand the Burmese explanations for each phrasal verb

## Phrasal Verbs

|-------------|---------|---------|
| bow to | အလျော့ပေးရသည်၊ လက်လွှတ်ရသည်။ | The company president finally bowed to pressure and resigned. |
| break down | (စက်)ပျက်သည်။ (ကိစ္စမရှိတဲ့အခြေအနေ)ယိုယွင်းသည်။ | Her health broke down under the pressure of work. |

| figure out | သဘောပေါက်သည်၊ နားလည်သည်၊ တွက်ချက်သည်။ | I couldn't figure out what the teacher was talking about. |
| go for sth | ရွေးချယ်သည်၊ ရယူရန် ကြိုးစားသည်။ | I think I'll go for the steak. What are you having? |
| go after | လိုက်သည်။ | He went after the burglars. |
| get back at | ကလည်စေချင်သည်။ | She was trying to get back at him for humiliating her. |
| hand in | တင်သပ်သည်၊ အပ်အပ်သည်၊ အပ်နှံသည်။ | I need to hand in my homework tomorrow. |
| hinge on | တည်သည်။ | My decision will hinge on your views. |
| look back | ပြန်လည်တူးဆွဲကြည့်သည်။ | Stop spending your time looking back. Live your life now! |

| look down on | အထင်အမြင်သေးသည်။ | She looks down on anyone who hasn't had a university education. |
| look forward to | မျှော်လင့်စောင့်စားသည်။ | We look forward to hearing from you soon. |
| pull down | ဖျက်ချသည်။ | The old warehouse has been pulled down. |
| read on | ဆက်ဖတ်သည်။ | Please read on to the end of the chapter. |
| read out | ဖတ်ပြသည်။ | He began to read out loud. |
| sum up | အကျဉ်းချုပ်သည်။ | I can't sum up his whole philosophy in one sentence. |
| shape up | တိုးတက်အားကောင်းစေသည်။ | I have been told that if I don't shape up, I will lose my job. |
| tell off | ဆူသည်၊ ကန့်သတ်သည်။ | The pupil was told off for being careless. |
| wear off | တဖြည်းဖြည်းချင် ပျက်ပြယ်သွားသည်။ | The pain is slowly wearing off. |
| wipe out | ချမ်းနှိမ်သည်၊ တိုက်ဖျက်သည်။ | The government is trying to wipe out drug trafficking. |

## Examples

### bow to
- The company president finally bowed to pressure and resigned.
- The government had to bow to public pressure.
- I can't agree with you but I bow to your greater experience and knowledge.

### break down
- Her health broke down under the pressure of work.
- The machine must break down at this busy hour.
- His van broke down.

### call on
- The human rights group has called on the US to end the death penalty.
- We sometimes have to call on outside expertise.
- The President paid a courtesy call on Emperor Akihito.

### chum up
- She chummed up with some girls from Bristol on holiday.
- They chummed up in a short time.
- It's good to see your children chumming up with mine so well.

### cross out
- He could cross out half the words because they were unnecessary.
- Two sentences were crossed out.
- He crossed out three wrong words.

### flick through
- In bookshop, I picked up a novel by a new writer and began to flick through it.
- I had a quick flick through your report.
- I had a flick through the catalogue while I was waiting.

### figure out
- I couldn't figure out what the teacher was talking about.
- Please figure out the total cost.
- We must figure out how to solve the problem.

### go for sth
- I think I'll go for the steak. What are you having?
- I think I will go for the fruit salad.
- She is going for the world record in the high jump.

### go after
- He went after the burglars.
- Go after him and apologize.
- He goes after every woman he meets.

### get back at
- She was trying to get back at him for humiliating her.
- I will get back at him one day.
- He will get back at you for this.

### hand in
- I need to hand in my homework tomorrow.
- Please hand in the document to me.
- We handed in a letter of protest.

### hinge on
- My decision will hinge on your views.
- The answers hinge on supply and demand.
- But elections hinge on shorter-term concerns.

### look back
- Stop spending your time looking back. Live your life now and enjoy every moment!
- Adults often look back on their childhood as a golden age.
- When I look back I can see where we went wrong.

### look after
- We look after the neighbours' cat while they are away.
- I will look after her child when she is on a business trip.
- It is a tradition that the young look after the old in their family.

### look round
- Let's look round this museum slowly. I think it will be interesting.
- Let's look round the town this afternoon.
- Have a look round the shop.

### look down on
- She looks down on anyone who hasn't had a university education.
- You should never look down on poor people.
- Never look down on yourself.

### look forward to
- We look forward to hearing from you soon.
- I always look forward to getting home to my wife and children.
- I look forward to seeing you again.

### pull down
- The old warehouse has been pulled down.
- It is easier to pull down than build.
- The house was pulled down and the site redeveloped.

### read on
- Please read on to the end of the chapter.
- I took a book with me to read on the train.
- If you like a good story, read on.

### read out
- He began to read out loud.
- She read out the letters to all of us.
- He read out the names of all the winners.

### sum up
- I can't sum up his whole philosophy in one sentence.
- To sum up, there are three main ways of tackling the problem...
- You have only 100 words in which to sum up his speech.

### shape up
- I have been told that if I don't shape up, I will lose my job.
- He will shape up into an excellent basketball player.
- Mark didn't shape up until he was thirty years old.

### tell off
- The pupil was told off for being careless.
- My parents told me off for coming home late.
- They were told off to clean the latrines.

### wear off
- The pain is slowly wearing off.
- The effects of the drug were wearing off.
- After the effectiveness of the vaccines wears off, the risk of actual infection returns.

### wipe out
- The government is trying to wipe out drug trafficking.
- One dose of penicillin can wipe out the infection.
- The plague once could wipe out a village.
