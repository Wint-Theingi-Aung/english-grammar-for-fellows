# Unit 41: Present Perfect with ever, never, just, already and yet

## Learning Objectives
- Use 'ever' in questions with the present perfect
- Use 'never' in negative sentences with the present perfect
- Use 'just' to emphasize something that happened very recently
- Use 'already' to talk about something that happened earlier than expected
- Use 'yet' to ask about or deny expected actions

## Ever
Use 'ever' in questions with the present perfect. It means "at any time in your life". Ever comes just before the past participle.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| ever + past participle (questions) | Have you ever worn a suit? |
| ever + past participle (questions) | Has she ever appeared on TV? |

## Never
Use 'never' in negative sentences in the present perfect. It means "at no time in your life". Never comes before the past participle.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| never + past participle (negative) | I have never been to London. |
| never + past participle (negative) | He has never done the shopping. |

## Just
Use 'just' with the present perfect to emphasize that something happened very recently. Just goes before the past participle.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| just + past participle | I have just washed my hair. |
| just + past participle | Has she just taken the rubbish out? |

## Already
Use 'already' to talk about something that has happened earlier than expected. Already usually goes just before the past participle.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| already + past participle | Don't buy any bread. I have already bought some. |
| already + past participle | They have already switched off the TV. |

## Yet
Use 'yet' to ask if something expected has happened or to say that it hasn't. It is used in questions or negative sentences. Yet usually goes at the end of a sentence or clause.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| yet at end of question | Have you spoken to the teacher yet? |
| yet at end of negative | I haven't read the book yet. |
| yet at end of negative | She hasn't arrived yet. |
