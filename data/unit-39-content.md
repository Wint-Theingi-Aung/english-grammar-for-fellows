# Unit 39: Some, Any, A/An

## Learning Objectives
- Use "some" with uncountable nouns and plural countable nouns in affirmative sentences
- Use "any" with uncountable nouns and plural countable nouns in negative sentences and questions
- Use "a/an" with singular countable nouns
- Use "an" before vowel sounds

## Some

We use "some" with uncountable nouns and with plural countable nouns, in affirmative sentences.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Uncountable (affirmative) | We have got some water. |
| Plural countable (affirmative) | I have got some apples. |

## Any

We use "any" with uncountable nouns and with plural countable nouns, in negative sentences and questions.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Uncountable (negative) | I haven't got any milk. |
| Plural countable (negative) | He hasn't got any apples. |
| Uncountable (question) | Have you got any orange juice? |
| Plural countable (question) | Are there any bananas? |

## A/An

We use "a/an" with singular countable nouns in affirmative and negative sentences and in questions. "An" goes before a vowel sound.

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Singular countable (negative) | I haven't got a burger. |
| Singular countable (affirmative) | He has got an egg. |
| Before vowel sound (question) | Have you got an MP3 player? |
