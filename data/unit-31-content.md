# Unit 31: No sooner … than

## Learning Objectives
- Learn to combine two sentences using "No sooner … than"
- Use the correct auxiliary: did + V1 for past simple, had + V3 for past perfect
- Preserve the correct tense sequence when rewriting sentences

## No sooner … than

No sooner … than is used to talk about something that happens immediately after something else. It has two patterns depending on the tense.

### Pattern 1: No sooner + did + subject + V1 + than + sentence

Use this pattern when both actions are in the simple past tense.

| Two Sentences | Combined with No sooner … than |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| The driver saw a green signal. He started his car. | No sooner did the driver see a green signal than he started his car. |
| I finished my homework. I helped my mother. | No sooner did I finish my homework than I helped my mother. |

### Pattern 2: No sooner + had + subject + V3 + than + sentence

Use this pattern when the first action happened before the second (past perfect + simple past).

| Two Sentences | Combined with No sooner … than |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| As the bell had rung, the boys rushed to the ground. | No sooner had the bell rung than the boys rushed to the ground. |
| They started clapping as we had entered the room. | No sooner had we entered the room than they started clapping. |

## Attention
- "No sooner" comes at the beginning of the combined sentence.
- The auxiliary verb (did/had) comes before the subject.
- Use "than" (not "then") to connect the two clauses.
- Preserve the original tense: use did + V1 for simple past, had + V3 for past perfect.
